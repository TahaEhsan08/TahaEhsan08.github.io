01 · About

Why do cells stop listening?

I'm a first-year Life Sciences student at McMaster who wants to understand how tumours start, grow and spread, and to help the labs working out how to stop them.

My dad has a chordoma, a rare tumour that grows along the spine and skull base. Watching him go through it showed me how much we still don't know about rare cancers, and how much new research matters to the families waiting on it. He's the reason I want to work in tumour research.

That same drive led me to Relay for Life, where I helped raise $2,500 for cancer research and support, and it's why cell growth and division were always the topics I wanted to dig into when tutoring biology.

I bring the habits that make a reliable lab volunteer: careful work under pressure from years in a busy restaurant, following strict procedures as an Elections Canada officer, and explaining complex ideas clearly as a DECA trainer and tutor.

02 · Research interests

Inside the tumour.

The areas of cancer biology I want to learn about and contribute to as an undergraduate researcher, with a special interest in rare tumours like chordoma.

  • Growth

    Tumour biology

    How mutations let cancer cells ignore the signals that normally control growth and division.

  • Environment

    Tumour microenvironment

    How blood vessels, immune cells and surrounding tissue help a tumour survive, or fight it.

  • Tissue

    Histopathology

    Reading stained tissue, like the H&E slide behind my name, to diagnose and grade tumours.

  • Treatment

    Translational oncology

    Turning lab findings into better therapies and outcomes for patients.

What I'm looking for
